The premise

What if a civilization had version history?

Aspiland treats governance, culture and technology as things that should be understandable, reviewable and reversible. The repository preserves the strange, ambitious history of the project while separating current rules from proposals, experiments and archives.

01

History without confusion

Old ideas remain visible, but age does not make them binding or correct.

02

Change with evidence

Consequential changes should state their purpose, impact, risks and rollback path.

03

Participation by contribution

Read, question, document, test, translate, design or build. The first useful contribution matters more than a ceremonial label.

Repository architecture

One world. Clearly separated layers.

/canon

Accepted rules

Only explicitly accepted and effective documents belong here.

 /rfc

Proposals and decisions

Reviewable ideas with status, rationale, risks and history.

 /projects

Living experiments

Software, research and creative systems with bounded scope.

 /protocols

How change happens

Procedures for consent, review, continuity and rollback.

 /public

Prepared for publication

Reviewed communication material; public visibility does not create a blanket reuse license.

 /archive

Memory, not authority

Historical and superseded material preserved with context.
