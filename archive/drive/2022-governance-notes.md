# Sanitized Google Drive governance notes

Status: historical source summary; non-canonical  
Source period: 2022–2025  
Reviewed: 2026-07-12

## Purpose

This document preserves useful ideas recovered from a private Google Drive folder associated with the early Aspiland constitutional experiment. It is a curated summary, not a copy of the source files and not a declaration of current law.

No private Drive links, credentials, personal data or raw operational records are included.

## Source set reviewed

The reviewed set contained:

- an early constitutional spreadsheet and two later copies;
- a short preamble stub;
- two early role-application drafts;
- a nearby research note about arranging human needs by urgency.

The spreadsheets mixed definitions, unfinished rules, role permissions, change requests, workflow states and product-backlog ideas. Their structure is historically useful even where the individual statements are obsolete.

## Ideas worth preserving

### Governance lifecycle

The source distinguished development, review or waiting periods, and production. The durable idea is that consequential changes should move through explicit states, have a stated activation date and remain challengeable before taking effect.

### Notice and understandable participation

Users should be informed about upcoming changes and able to understand their rights and duties without specialist knowledge. Proposals should be available in a participant's preferred language when practical, while clearly identifying the authoritative source text.

### Consistency checking

The source proposed automatic detection of contradictions in changes. Automation may assist reviewers by finding conflicting definitions, incompatible permissions, missing activation dates and unresolved references, but it must not make the final governance decision.

### Continuity without permanent authority

The early material tried to protect continuity when an administrator became inactive. The useful requirement is continuity through documented succession, multiple maintainers, recoverable access and time-limited emergency authority. No role should require an unlimited personal commitment or expand itself without review.

### Sensory accessibility and personal boundaries

The source treated excessive noise, light, smell, proximity and interruption as governance concerns. This survives as a general accessibility principle: shared spaces and systems should respect declared sensory boundaries, consent and reasonable accommodation.

### Transparent criteria and appeal

Assessments and restrictions should use clear criteria. Affected people should receive reasons, have an opportunity to respond and be able to request review.

### Data and access safety

The source called for secure personal-data storage and reliable file access. The current interpretation is data minimization, purpose limitation, access control, recoverability, deletion rules and explicit separation between public and private material.

### Onboarding and operational handover

Applications, role requests and operational procedures should be understandable, available in accessible language and supported by practical handover documentation. Joining a project must not imply surrendering autonomy or accepting indefinite duties.

### Needs-first design

A nearby research note emphasized that essential physiological and safety needs precede higher-order participation. Aspiland can preserve this as a design heuristic: governance should not demand complex participation from people whose immediate safety, access or basic needs are unresolved.

### Economy as an experiment

The source sketched a token, budget and recurring-distribution model. This may be useful as a simulation under `projects/`, but it should not determine identity, access to essential services, ownership or membership.

## Material intentionally not imported

The following categories were excluded from the migration:

- credentials, keys and infrastructure secrets;
- private or unnecessary personal information;
- coercive or indefinite loyalty commitments;
- irreversible sanctions and automatic exclusion or property transfer;
- rules that ignore consent, safety, privacy or fair process;
- duplicate copies, placeholders and incomplete sentences;
- copied reference text that did not add original Aspiland content.

## Mapping to the current repository

- Change lifecycle and activation already align with `protocols/change-control.md`.
- Consent, privacy, proportionality and reviewability align with the current historical constitution and RFC 0003.
- The remaining actionable ideas are collected as a proposed implementation backlog in `rfc/0004-governance-automation-backlog.md`.

## Historical interpretation

The Drive material is evidence of early design exploration. It should be used to recover promising questions and experiments, not to restore superseded rules or bypass the current RFC and review process.
