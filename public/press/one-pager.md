## What is Aspiland?

Aspiland is an open repository and long-running laboratory for digital governance, software, artificial life, research and creative experiments.

It explores a simple question:

> **What if a civilization had version history?**

The repository preserves the project's unusual history while making an important distinction between accepted rules, proposals, working procedures, software experiments, material prepared for publication and archives.

Aspiland is an experiment, not a sovereign state, legal jurisdiction or production public service.

## Why does it exist?

Many systems hide how rules changed, who proposed them, what assumptions they rely on and how mistakes can be reversed. Aspiland tests a different approach:

- preserve history without treating every historical idea as current;
- make consequential proposals inspectable and challengeable;
- separate governance from fiction, research and software experiments;
- document risks, authority, uncertainty and rollback paths;
- value useful contribution over ceremonial membership labels.

## How is the repository organized?

- `canon/` — explicitly accepted and effective documents;
- `rfc/` — proposals and durable decision records;
- `protocols/` — recurring procedures for review, consent, continuity and rollback;
- `projects/` — bounded software, research and creative experiments;
- `public/` — material reviewed for unrestricted public communication;
- `archive/` — historical and superseded material preserved with context.

A document is not binding merely because it is old, public, merged or written in constitutional language.

## What can people do now?

During Repository Preview, people can:

- read and inspect the repository;
- question assumptions and terminology;
- report safety, privacy, accessibility or clarity problems;
- improve documentation and translations;
- test or document a bounded project;
- submit an RFC or another reviewable contribution.

The project is not yet inviting people into an operating governed community or shared public service.

## Current status

The repository has a clearer structure, glossary, contribution process, code of conduct, security-reporting procedure and change-control baseline.

Important launch work remains open, including comprehensive secret and personal-data review, licensing decisions, recovery testing, project ownership, repository controls and a minimal accepted canonical package for any future community launch.

The honest current label is:

> **Repository Preview — an open prototype of a digital civilization.**

## Selected concepts

### First Commit Kit

A plain-language path that helps a new reader understand the repository and make a small, useful, reversible first contribution.

### System Guardian

A bounded operational and advisory role that may observe authorized resources, propose changes, perform reversible maintenance and narrowly contain active security or privacy exposure. It is not ownership, sovereignty, legal agency or unlimited administration.

### APS and historical economy experiments

Historical tokens and accounting concepts are experiments or metaphors. They have no guaranteed financial value, entitlement or connection to essential access.

### Embassies

Historical organizational metaphors for project spaces or connections. They do not have diplomatic status, immunity or territorial authority.

### Muminki and artificial-life work

Software and research experiments concerned with simulated worlds, memory, behavior and artificial life. Project claims must be evaluated within their documented implementation and status.

## Core message

> **A world you do not join through a form. You enter through a commit.**

Aspiland does not ask readers to believe that the world is complete. It invites them to inspect the evidence, challenge the drafts and leave the repository clearer than they found it.
