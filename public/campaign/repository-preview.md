# Repository Preview campaign

Status: public campaign draft; non-canonical  
Last reviewed: 2026-07-12

## Goal

Introduce Aspiland as an inspectable experimental repository without implying a sovereign state, operating governed community, production service or completed technology platform.

The campaign should create curiosity, direct people to evidence and invite small reviewable contributions.

## Audience

Primary:

- open-source contributors;
- developers and researchers interested in digital governance or artificial life;
- designers, writers and translators interested in experimental worldbuilding;
- people who care about transparent rules, reversible change and public decision history.

Secondary:

- journalists, educators and potential partners looking for an unusual but clearly bounded digital-culture project;
- former participants who may help classify historical material.

## Core proposition

> **Aspiland is an open prototype of a digital civilization whose history, proposals and experiments can be inspected in a public repository.**

## Campaign line

> **A world you do not join through a form. You enter through a commit.**

Polish:

> **Świat, do którego nie zapisujesz się formularzem. Wchodzisz przez commit.**

## Required boundary

Every campaign destination must make this visible:

> Aspiland is an experiment, not a sovereign state, legal jurisdiction or production public service.

Do not shorten it in a way that removes the distinction.

## Phase 2 — Reveal

Purpose: explain what Aspiland is and is not.

### Main announcement

Aspiland is open for Repository Preview.

It is a long-running laboratory for digital governance, software, artificial life, research and creative experiments. Its repository separates accepted rules from proposals, projects, public material and archives.

This is not the launch of a sovereign state or operating governed community. It is an invitation to inspect the evidence, question the drafts and improve a bounded part of the world.

**A world you do not join through a form. You enter through a commit.**

Repository: `https://github.com/lbabut/aspiland`

## Phase 3 — First contributors

Purpose: replace vague calls to join with concrete contribution paths.

### Invitation post

Aspiland does not need ceremonial sign-ups. It needs useful first contributions.

You can:

- clarify one confusing term;
- document one active experiment;
- repair one misleading link;
- test one setup path;
- improve one accessibility gap;
- challenge one proposal with evidence.

Read the First Commit Kit and leave the repository clearer than you found it.

### First commit recognition

**FIRST COMMIT RECORDED**

A reviewed contribution made Aspiland clearer, safer or more useful.

The contribution history is the certificate. No legal identity, financial value, ownership or governance authority is implied.

## Recurring series — Documents intercepted from Aspiland

Each item must be clearly labelled as current explanation, historical archive, proposal, fiction or satire.

### 1. The constitution has version history

Angle: rules should show where they came from, what changed and how a mistake can be reversed.

Call to action: inspect `canon/`, `rfc/` and the change-control protocol.

### 2. Memory is not authority

Angle: preserving old documents does not make every old idea current or correct.

Call to action: help classify a legacy area.

### 3. Citizenship by contribution?

Angle: historical citizen language is a metaphor; current participation is voluntary and a contribution grants no legal identity.

Call to action: use the First Commit Kit.

### 4. Who guards the system?

Angle: explain the bounded System Guardian role, its permissions and exclusions.

Call to action: read RFC 0005 and its appointment record.

### 5. Does a digital world need money?

Angle: explain APS and Hajs as historical opt-in experiments without guaranteed value or entitlement.

Call to action: help document the economy experiment safely and accurately.

### 6. What lives inside Muminki?

Angle: show artificial-life and simulated-world experiments while separating implemented behavior from mythology or future ambition.

Call to action: improve project status and safe run instructions.

### 7. An embassy without territory

Angle: historical embassy language describes connection or project space, not diplomatic status or immunity.

Call to action: document the provenance and current status of an embassy record.

### 8. Can a world roll back a bad decision?

Angle: show why reversibility, effective dates and review windows matter.

Call to action: review a proposed protocol or RFC.

## Publication checklist

Before each campaign item:

- [ ] Link to a current repository source.
- [ ] Confirm the source status and review date.
- [ ] Include the experiment disclaimer where the format could imply statehood or a service.
- [ ] Do not expose personal data, secrets or private infrastructure.
- [ ] Do not imply financial value, legal identity or automatic authority.
- [ ] Label historical, proposed, fictional and accepted material correctly.
- [ ] Check readability, alt text and keyboard-accessible destination pages.
- [ ] Record any factual correction in the source material, not only in the social post.
