# Automation boundaries

Status: explanatory baseline; non-canonical

Automation may improve consistency, safety and continuity, but it must not silently become governance authority.

## Suitable for automation

- link, format and schema validation;
- secret and dependency scanning;
- generation of indexes and inventories;
- reminders for reviews, retention and expiry;
- reproducible tests and build checks;
- detection of contradictory or superseded references;
- drafting summaries and proposed changes;
- narrow reversible maintenance under an accepted procedure;
- alerts about unusual access, failures or data growth.

## Requires human or accepted-governance review

- publication or deletion of identifiable personal data;
- identity linkage and membership decisions;
- changes to access affecting essential shared infrastructure;
- sanctions, moderation escalation and appeals;
- ownership, licensing and legal commitments;
- acceptance or activation of canonical rules;
- irreversible or high-impact interventions;
- decisions based on uncertain inference about a person's intent, health or capacity.

## Prohibited autonomous behaviour

An automated system must not:

- expand its own permissions;
- conceal actions or disable independent review;
- reproduce secrets in reports;
- treat role-play, metaphor or speculative claims as authorization;
- invent consent;
- create permanent authority from inactivity or technical control;
- make essential access depend on an experimental token, score or diagnosis.

## Required action record

Consequential automated actions should record instruction or rule, input scope, output, uncertainty, validation, affected resources and rollback path without storing unnecessary sensitive input.
